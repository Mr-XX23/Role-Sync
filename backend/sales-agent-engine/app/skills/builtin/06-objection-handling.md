# Objection handling

Goal: a response that takes the objection seriously, answers it with evidence and moves the deal forward.

## Steps
1. Name the objection: **price or budget**, **timing**, **competitor**, **no need or status quo**, **authority**, or **risk and trust**. If it hides another one (for example "too expensive" meaning "not convinced of the value"), say so.
2. Find evidence: battlecards, case studies with results, and pricing guidance with `search_knowledge_base`; `recall` what this account cares about; check the deal with `search_deals`.
3. For price, look up the item's discount limits with `search_catalog`. Never offer more than the limit, and prefer trading value (term, volume, scope) over discounts.

## Response pattern
1. **Acknowledge**: show you understand the concern, without agreeing it is a blocker.
2. **Clarify**: one question that uncovers what is really behind it.
3. **Respond**: one or two proof points specific to them (numbers, a similar customer).
4. **Confirm and advance**: check the concern is addressed, and propose a concrete next step.

## Output
- The type of objection and what is likely behind it.
- A spoken response for a call, three to five sentences.
- A written version of the same for email.
- The question to ask next, and what to do if the answer is still no.

Never disparage a competitor or promise something the product doesn't do.
